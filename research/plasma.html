Why satellites should breathe

Global plasma modelling for very low Earth orbit

From reactions to thrust

The collective model closes particle and energy balances for a multi-species nitrogen–oxygen discharge, then converts the plasma state into extracted current, efficiency and thrust.

The result

Benchmarked against xenon and experimental nitrogen–oxygen trends, the model identifies a narrow feasibility window around 200–250 km under explicit intake, power and drag assumptions.

The value

The solver runs in seconds: fast enough to map a design space before committing to spatial plasma simulations or hardware.

The limit

This is a feasibility model, not a flight qualification. Spatial losses, atmospheric variability, compression, surfaces and mission geometry remain decisive.

THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Can the atmosphere that slows a satellite also become its propellant?

Methods

  • Coupled particle and energy balance equations
  • Nine-species plasma kinetics
  • Benchmark validation
  • Parameter sweeps and performance modelling
