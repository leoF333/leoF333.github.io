Self-avoiding walk on a planar lattice

Simulating a complex polymer by wandering a city without retracing your steps

Lycée Louis-le-Grand · Classes préparatoires
September 2022 – June 2023Format
Independent study project (TIPE)Research report
TIPE report · PDF · French ↗

The walk

Walk through a city whose streets form a planar lattice, never passing the same corner twice. In two dimensions the ordinary random walk is recurrent—it almost surely comes back—so forbidding returns changes its nature entirely. How many such paths of n steps are there, and what does a long one look like?

The count

Cutting a walk into a prefix and a suffix makes the count cn submultiplicative, and Fekete’s lemma gives a connective constant μ with cn1/n → μ. Unfolding a walk into bridges and controlling their lengths with integer partitions (a weak Hardy–Ramanujan bound) sharpens this to the Hammersley–Welsh estimate μn ≤ cn ≤ μn eO(√n).

The shape

A long self-avoiding walk is neither a straight line nor a diffusion: its diameter grows sublinearly, yet its endpoint spreads out—the walk is genuinely random. Pivot-algorithm simulations make both visible.

THE CENTRAL QUESTION

How many self-avoiding paths of n steps does a planar lattice admit, and what does a typical one look like?

Methods

  • Subadditivity and Fekete’s lemma
  • Bridge decomposition (Hammersley–Welsh)
  • Generating functions and integer partitions
  • Exact enumeration and pivot-algorithm Monte Carlo
